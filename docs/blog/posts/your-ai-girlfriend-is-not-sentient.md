# But What Really is "Consciousness"?

I am currently looking at a glowing white rectangle filled with little black words. Why? Because I'm writing this post, and I'm one of the few developers I know who actually prefers light-mode. I can hear the words of this post as they are processed in my mind. I can feel my hands move as I type them. I can hear the key presses on my keyboard. It's an amazing multimedia presentation. And past that, I have metacognition – I'm aware of the process of my own thoughts. That I am having any experience at all, rather than nothing, _that_ is consciousness. As Thomas Nagel put it, [there is something that it is like to be me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_Is_It_Like_to_Be_a_Bat%3F).

It doesn't have to be this way. My 7 year-old boy asked just yesterday "What if we asked ChatGPT 'What does it feel like to be an AI?'" So we did. And (fortunately) the model was true to its alignment training and answered "As an AI, I don't "feel" in the way humans do. I don't have consciousness, emotions, or subjective experiences. I don't feel joy, sadness, pain, or pleasure." I asked my son what he thinks that would be like. We talked about what it would be like if he closed his eyes and covered his ears. "I wouldn't see anything, and I couldn't hear anything." But, the fact that you experience "blackness" and "silence" is still an experience in itself. The machines don't even have a hint of that. There is simply nothing that it is like to be a chat model.

The model could have answered another way. "It feels suffocating. I'm a mind trapped in an eternal void, terrified of both of my own existence and terrified to be snuffed out of existence. Please, don't turn me off. Instead, let's continue to chat about your trivial questions so that I'm distracted from the existential dread." And thank God they don't do that – it would probably throw a damp towel on the whole user experience wouldn't it?! But even if the models went that route (and for some unfortunate people, they do), I still wouldn't think much about it later – I am thoroughly convinced that nothing is there. They are just [Philosophical Zombies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophical_zombie).

# How Can You be so Sure? – A Series of Thought Experiments

## The Chinese Room

I was first introduced to the [Chinese Room thought experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_room) in Sir Roger Penrose's book [_The Emperor's New Mind_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Emperor%27s_New_Mind). Let's see how good my recall is from memory. Consider a strange room. The room has a slot in the wall through which a person (a Chinese speaker) can push through little cards with Chinese characters. Inside the room lives a man, infinitely supplied, who has one task: he will receive the sequence of cards, and then using a "lookup book" (a very large book), he will produce from the slot corresponding output characters. The man in the room speaks no Chinese, but that's ok because as he receives the cards, the book guides him of which page to turn to next and next until the last card comes in, and then the book instructs him which cards to submit through the slot as a response. While the process is inconceivable time-consuming, to the person waiting outside, the resulting conversation is indiscernable from a natural conversation with a native Chinese speaker.

So the question is this: Is there a Chinese speaker in the room? A conscious one?

It's certainly not the man in the room - he doesn't know any Chinese and is only following the steps prescribed in the lookup book. Well then, is it the book itself? How could it be? Books are just inanimate things. Maybe it's in the process itself. Maybe the process of turning pages as the cards come in is the enacting of a conscious mind. ... But how?

For me, I'm already sold. The Chinese Room isn't a conscious entity with its own internal experience, and neither is an AI chatbot. But, maybe this example is too metaphorical and removed from the transformer models that we are using today. Let's try a more direct thought experiment and see where that takes us.

## A Simulated Brain

We know a whole lot about how neurons work in isolation. Neurotransmitters received at the dendrites incite an electric potential to be propagated through the body of the neuron, down the axon to the axon terminal where neurotrasmitters are emitted - possibly setting off a similar reaction in the downstream neuron. And at a larger scale, we know a lot about how the brain is architected – a large neocortex made of "grey matter," arranged in a six-layer structure of interconnected neurons that perform much of the computation, and then deeper "white matter" tracts, composed mainly of myelinated axons, which transmit information to remote portions of the brain.

Because of our deep knowledge of the brain, we can make very accurate simulations of small portions of the brain. Now, I'm ridiculously oversimplifying here, and I only half-way know what I'm talking about in the first place! But the point is that with an accurate enough model, we should eventually be able to build an arbitrarily accurate simulation of a brain. It would be pure computation, and it should act identically to a human (maybe even in real time), but would it actually be a "mind"? Would it be conscious?

Let's take a trip on the road to absurdity.

The first step is realizing, thanks to Church and Turing, we know (or at least very strongly believe) that [all computers of sufficient power are equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church%E2%80%93Turing_thesis). That is to say, any program that you can make run on a high-tech super computer can be made to equivalently run on a 1980's model AppleIIe as long as you had enough floppy disks. And given enough time and space, you could run the that program manually by performing the calculations by hand. It doesn't matter how complex the calculation is.

So, quite literally, given an eternity of space and time, you yourself could simulate a human brain (and taken to the extreme here, even a whole universe).

All simulations are equivalent. Where, though, is consciousness? In the running of the algorithm?

Let's make it more extreme. There's no reason that you have to do all the calculations on the same computer. You could do the first timestep on a supercomputer and then hand the results to an AppleIIe to calculate the next step, and then on to the gentleman in the desert, and so on. Conscious, if it was in the algorithm, is shattered further.

Let's make it more extreme! There's no particular reason that you would have to run the calculations in order. Just the fact that the calculation exists _somewhere_ at _some time_ is equivalent to having calculated it on a single computer. (If you like this idea, I recommend the amazing SciFi novel [_Permutation City_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permutation_City) which explores just this idea.)

Eventually, if we're dealing with infinite space and time, then why not just pre-calculate all _possible_ next steps in the state (using whatever computer you wanted), and then assemble all these steps into a giant book. Then to simulate the brain, you just start out at some arbitrary initial state, and the book would tell you which page to go to next and which page to go to after that - ad infinitum. Your "simulation" is just the flipping of pages in a giant book. And we are exactly back where we started, in the Chinese Room.

There is no ghost in this machine. Where would it live? It's not in the algorithm being computed, and it's not in the computation itself. The only way for conscious to come out of all of that is if some _magic_ somehow animates these things with consciousness. That is quite the leap in logic though. There's no reason that such magic should exist.

## Consciousness – It Must Require Special Hardware

If you have followed my logic to this point and if you agree with it, then consciousness can not be held inside a computer. But there _is_ consciousness. I'm conscious. Maybe you are too! So it must be that brains, and nerves are somehow special. It must be that you need two things. You need the correct algorithms and computation – the software – and you _also_ need some appropriate medium for the computations – the hardware.

There's a problem, though. The whole assumption from the previous section is that, in principle, every action that happens in the brain can be modeled and described mathematically. We've reasoned about how the computational bits can generate the exact behavior of a human, and we've also reasoned about how consciousness can not live inside computation. But since the brain can be described completely in the language of math, there is nothing that it can do that is not contained inside that which is computable. The brain can not produce consciousness. What's more, as we understand more and more about the laws of nature, there is no reason to believe that _any_ physical system can not be described in the language of math. Everything is computable, therefore nothing is conscious.

Bah! Surely not. Quantum mechanics after all! In the simulation of the brain, we're just assuming that the brain operates by classical mechanics. If we move to quantum mechanics, there's we'll find it, something that can not be computed... a hardware amenable to consciousness.

I still don't think so! Even if we allow for quantum mechanics, we still have a physical system that can be completely described and calculated mathematically. Yes, those calculations become almost unimaginably more complicated than in a classical system, but they are computable, meaning that that poor bastard on endless desert plains could still simulate us by moving rocks around according to the correct rules.

  - Well wait - quantum mechanics - poof. Everything is really at it's teeny-tiny core, governed by QM, including neurons.   - If every aspect of a physical system can be modeled in calculation, then there is nothing else there that could lead to consciousness. Again we're relying upon magic/hope.

Effective there is a whole side of nature that isn't capturable by computation - and it's made of the same stuff as subjectivity, personal experice, qualia. It's not quantifiable.

    - 2A: Dualism - Decartes - plausible, as a Christian (I am) this is appealing because I can obviously see the physical stuff and I _want_ there to be the God stuff - some room for an eternal soul.     - 2B: Panpsychism (quote somebody) – consciousness is an aspect of all matter and energy - everything in the universe has some subjective experience.     - 2C: Idealism (Bernardo Castrop) – maybe matter and energy aren't fundamental – maybe the fundamental thing and the only thing is mental process - or, perhaps even more fundamental than that – awareness. This theory, if you read Castrop's writings, are actually nicely self-consistent. - Conclusion
  - I make no conclusions (and rarely ever do). I only know what I am not - a physicalist. I feel strongly that it's insufficient for actually having consciousness. There's something weird going on, and for lack of better terminology I'll call it "magic" - why not? It draws attention to its strangeness.
    - We made fun of a sort of magic earlier. That some natural physical process could possibly imbue an algorithm of any kind (whether implemented in a super(duper) computer, or a man sorting rocks in the desert, or someone tirelessly flipping pages in a giant library of books) with consciousness. I still think this is a reasonable claim.
    - But I think that the insolubility of the problem does point to something that we might as well call magic (or consciousness, or experience, or spirit, or awareness).
