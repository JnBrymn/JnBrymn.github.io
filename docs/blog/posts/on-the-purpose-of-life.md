I always thought that some bible verses make God sound a bit prideful.

> For my own sake, for my own sake, I do this.
> How can I let myself be defamed?
> I will not yield my glory to another.
>
> — Isaiah 48:11 (NIV)

But I always reasoned to myself that, hey, if God really created this universe, and really is the omnipotent totality of all power and authority in the universe, then sure, He – singularly – deserves all that glory. So, it's not a matter of pride.

But maybe I was interpreting these verses incorrectly.

--- 

Meanwhile, here I am on earth trying to figure out what I'm supposed to be doing with my time. If you'd asked me when I was a kid, I think I would have told you that I was going to be a scientist. Why? Because science interests me, and the purpose of my little life was to follow my interests. If you'd asked me the same question when I was in college, then I would have said that I wanted to build a startup. Not only would I be able to do something "important", but I would gain status, recognition, and wealth! Yahoo! Here I am in my mid 40's, and my answer is more subdued and even a bit vague. I would like to do _something_ to bring value in the world, I would like to be a good father and husband, and when given the opportunity, maybe I can help some people out. I measure my success by the fact that people have been willing to pay me for my skills, my wife seems happy, and my kids are growing up to be good people. Maybe someday I'll still go for that startup. At least at this point I've started to set my own ego aside. If I did have a startup, I wouldn't care as much about the prestige – I just want to make something that people find helpful.

But it feels like something is missing. What of the money that I make? I'll set it aside when I die. And it's nice to build things that people use; but none of my contributions will be important enough to remember in a century (and more likely, far less). I love my family, and they provide a sense of purpose to my life; but this is a pretty typical purpose – everyone who has a spouse should be a good partner to them, and everyone who has kids should raise them well. I – everyone – longs for a special purpose.

---

Today's sermon at church was great, I was given a completely different read on God's glory, and linked it to a new idea of purpose that hadn't sunk in until today.

It's not that my early read was entirely incorrect. When you create the whole of the cosmos, then really, all glory is due you – you have a solid claim! But _why_ is it so important for God to be glorified? That's where I had it wrong. God doesn't need anything from us, and in particular, He's not demanding our praise in order to boulster His own ego.

> And he is not served by human hands, as if he needed anything. Rather, he himself gives everyone life and breath and everything else.
>
> - Acts 17:25 (NIV)

Rather, God is the source of all fulfillment, and He offers his fulfillment to all of us.

> Jesus answered, "Everyone who drinks this water will be thirsty again, but whoever drinks the water I give them will never thirst. Indeed, the water I give them will become in them a spring of water welling up to eternal life."
>
> — John 4:13–14 (NIV)

He sees us chasing around temporary goals – some well meaning, some rather egotistical – He tolerates it, but He knows that He has something better for us, something eternal.

 > Why spend money on what is not bread,                                  
 > and your labor on what does not satisfy?                             
 > 
 > — Isaiah 55:2 (NIV)

So, the glory that we give to God is a light pointing others toward the love and purpose that God offers all of us.

> In the same way, let your light shine before others, that they may see your good deeds and glorify your Father in heaven.
>
> Matthew 5:16 (NIV)

For me, this was a completely new read over verses that I had heard all of my life and never really understood fully.

---

That's where we finally get to purpose. In the sermon, the minister said very concisely, "the purpose of your life is to bring glory to God".

If you take God to be human-like in nature, egotistical, then this sounds very self-serving. But if you hold true that God is the source of all love and all fulfillment, then the glory we give to God points others to him and it aligns our own focus upon Him. Our praise toward God is beneficial to us. It is a recognition that we are not the center of the universe and our recognition that our focus is upon the only thing that is permanent, the one thing we take with us. Therefore:

> So whether you eat or drink or whatever you do, do it all for the glory of God.
>
> — 1 Corinthians 10:31 (NIV)

There are practical and immediate benefits of this realization. If I've found the source of all love and fulfillment, then I don't have to look for anything else to satisfy me. Why chase after wealth, status, and power when you have all along known that those will be dropped at the door when you leave the room (life, that is)?

It also changes _how_ I pursue objectives in this world. It's not that I won't pursue _anything_ here. As a matter of fact, I might pursue the very same things – raising a family, being a good husband, building things that people find useful – but I pursue them differently. I know they are not the end goal, they are just a means of experiencing the glory of God, and projecting my gratitude to Him, and showing his glory to others.

And if "the purpose of your life is to bring glory to God", then this changes your expectations quite a bit. If my purported purpose is "to build great things", but it just never works out, then I have failed at life. But if my purpose is to bring glory to God, and I want to do it by building great things, then it's nice when things work out, but the outcome actually doesn't matter. If I try hard and fail, it doesn't have anything to do with my purpose. I could lose status, health, and wealth and _still_ bring glory to God because of the way I handle the situation.
